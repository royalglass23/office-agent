# Client response patterns and next actions

Derived from the authorised ServiceM8 historical review on 23 October 2026. These are newly written,
de-identified draft patterns, not verbatim customer messages, confirmed company terms or messages
ready to send without checking the selected job. See [findings](history-findings.md).

## Communication style observed

The enhanced pass processed all 26,505 outbound emails; 26,435 had nonempty cleaned text.
16,920 of those began with "Hi" under the greeting heuristic (about 64%). Median cleaned length
was 48 words and the 90th percentile 146 words. Outbound data includes supplier correspondence and
templates, not just individually written client responses. Signature/thread stripping is heuristic.

Useful recurring structures in checked client examples:

- Acknowledge the enquiry, update or concern.
- Answer the actual question before adding background.
- State the specific information, decision or condition still needed.
- Explain why it matters in one plain sentence.
- End with one clear next step; use short numbered points for several distinct questions.
- Distinguish estimate, proposed date and confirmed commitment.

Technical explanations, complaints and complex scope changes need more space than a simple
follow-up. Word-count statistics are descriptive, not a required response limit.

## Draft patterns

### RP-01: Missing project information

**Use when:** the current product-specific checklist identifies information genuinely missing.

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you for your enquiry about [product]. To prepare [an indicative estimate / the next review],
> could you send [the missing plan or photo], [approximate dimensions] and [the missing site details]?
>
> This will help us check the scope and advise the next step.

**Recommended action:** consolidate unanswered questions; assign the information request to the
job coordinator; set a follow-up using the approved timing. Do not ask again for information
already provided in an accessible message or attachment. Supports HR-02/03.

### RP-02: Quote follow-up

**Use when:** the correct quote/revision has been identified and an approved follow-up is due.

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm following up on the [product/work] quotation we sent on [verified date]. Have you had a chance
> to review it, and is there anything you would like us to clarify?
>
> If your project timing has changed, please let us know so we can plan the next step with you.

**Recommended action:** check for acceptance, decline, a revised quote, a later reply and an agreed
hold before preparing this. Escalate missing history rather than assert "no response". Supports HR-09.

### RP-03: Site readiness before final measure

**Use when:** the relevant product process requires finished surfaces or another readiness condition.

> Hi [Name],
>
> Before we arrange the final measurement for [product], could you confirm that [verified readiness
> requirement] is complete and send [the relevant photos]?
>
> Once our team has checked those details, we can confirm the next visit.

**Recommended action:** request readiness evidence and prepare a measure booking for staff review.
Do not insert shower-specific requirements into another product's process. Supports HR-04/08.

### RP-04: Drawing or scope conflict

**Use when:** two sources disagree on an identifiable detail.

> Hi [Name],
>
> Before we proceed, could you confirm [specific detail]? The [drawing/version] shows [A], while
> [the other verified source] shows [B].
>
> We can finalise the next step once the agreed design is confirmed and any required review is complete.

**Recommended action:** identify the source versions, obtain written clarification and prepare a
technical/price review if needed. Do not silently pick the newest-looking drawing. Supports HR-06/10.

### RP-05: Acceptance and applicable deposit

**Use when:** acceptance and the applicable terms have been verified; the draft does not claim that
an invoice has been attached or issued unless that action really happened.

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you for confirming you would like to proceed. The next step is [verified next step]. The
> agreed deposit is [amount/percentage from the applicable accepted terms].
>
> Please [verified payment instruction / review the invoice once issued]. If payment has already
> been made, let us know and our accounts team can check it.

**Recommended action:** check existing payment information before a reminder, then prepare the
invoice or request an accounts check. Do not default every job to 50%. Supports HR-07.

### RP-06: Conditional installation update

**Use when:** staff have a genuine proposed window and can identify its outstanding conditions.

> Hi [Name],
>
> Our proposed installation window is [current staff-reviewed window], subject to [specific
> outstanding condition]. We are checking [material delivery / readiness / availability].
>
> We will confirm the booking once that is resolved. Please let us know if [relevant access detail]
> has changed.

**Recommended action:** identify the blocker, assign its owner and prepare the update. If there is
no supported window, say timing is being checked rather than invent a date. Supports HR-08/12.

### RP-07: Variation or changed specification

**Use when:** a change affects the agreed scope, price or delivery.

> Hi [Name],
>
> The requested change is [verified change]. We need to review its effect on [scope / materials /
> price / timing] before confirming the revised work.
>
> We will prepare [the revised quotation / variation] for your review and approval.

**Recommended action:** prepare the change summary and estimator review; identify the superseded
revision. Do not promise that the change is free or fits the existing date. Supports HR-06/10.

### RP-08: Issue or possible rectification

**Use when:** the client reports an issue that needs investigation.

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you for letting us know about [reported issue]. Could you send [the specific photo or detail
> needed] and confirm [relevant circumstance]?
>
> We will ask [the responsible team] to review this and advise the next step.

**Recommended action:** preserve the complaint, request targeted evidence and assign technical
review. Do not infer cause, fault, legal compliance or warranty coverage. Supports HR-11.

### RP-09: Client not ready

**Use when:** an actual client instruction places the project on hold or supplies a later timeframe.

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you for the update. We understand that [verified timeframe or hold instruction].
>
> [If appropriate: Would you like us to check back around [proposed date]?] Please let us know when
> you are ready to discuss the next step.

**Recommended action:** retain the reason and agreed follow-up date, or ask staff to choose one.
Do not keep sending ordinary quote reminders during a documented hold. Supports HR-09.

## Next-action record the agent should prepare

Each recommendation should contain:

| Field | Example of the required meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Situation | What the accessible current evidence establishes. |
| Next action | One specific task, such as confirm design or request readiness photos. |
| Reason and evidence | Why it is needed, with the relevant source and timestamp. |
| Responsible person | Verified owner, or clearly labelled proposed owner for staff confirmation. |
| Due-date basis | Agreed customer date or approved policy; do not invent an SLA. |
| Blocker / missing evidence | What prevents progress or prevents a reliable recommendation. |
| Draft | The appropriate pattern filled only with supported facts. |
| Approval | Who must approve the communication, technical decision, quote or booking. |

Recommendations are preparation for staff review. A historical example does not prove the same
customer is still waiting, that a product is suitable, or that a proposed action can run automatically.
