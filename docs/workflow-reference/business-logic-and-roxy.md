# Business logic and knowledge to collect from Roxy

Status: proposed collection framework, 23 October 2026. No rule below is a verified statement of
Roxy's actual practice. Timings, amounts, thresholds and authority limits must come from evidence
and confirmation, rather than being invented by the model.

## What the agent should help staff do

- Explain the current job situation and cite the relevant evidence.
- Identify missing information, blockers, unanswered messages and approaching commitments.
- Recommend the next action, the responsible person and an appropriate due date.
- Draft a client response using verified facts and approved wording.
- Prepare quote, booking, variation, invoice and closure proposals as tools become available.
- Explain uncertainty and ask a focused question when evidence cannot support a decision.

## Small exceptions that change the next action

- A client gives a specific date when they will respond.
- A quote is on hold or has been superseded.
- Materials are ordered but delivery is unconfirmed.
- Customer dimensions change after the quote.
- Site access or another trade prevents installation.
- Two staff members are each waiting for the other.
- A booked date is provisional rather than a confirmed customer commitment.
- A message requires a phone call, technical review or manager decision.
- A payment is partial, unreconciled or disputed.
- A completed job still has missing documents or unresolved rectification.

Ask how Roxy recognises and handles each situation. Do not infer the intended rule solely from
the final job status.

## Record each proposed rule consistently

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | The event or condition that makes the rule relevant. |
| Required evidence | Fields, messages, documents and timestamps that must be checked. |
| Conditions | Product/job/customer context and permitted exceptions. |
| Recommended action | What should happen and why. |
| Owner and due date | Who is responsible and how timing is calculated. |
| Draft guidance | Approved wording, facts to include and promises requiring confirmation. |
| Authority | Whether the agent can read, recommend, prepare or execute after approval. |
| Stop/escalate condition | Missing evidence, conflicts or decisions requiring another person. |
| Provenance | Supporting job/event references and observation dates. |
| Approval and version | Who confirmed the rule, when, and whether it supersedes an older rule. |

## Learning from historical jobs

History can reveal communication style, information requests, follow-up patterns, ownership,
recurring blockers and typical sequences. It may not explain why a decision was made, whether
phone/off-system discussions occurred, or whether an older practice is still approved.

Classify findings as:

1. **Explicit instruction:** a documented rule whose current authority and applicability still
   need to be established before it becomes company policy.
2. **Repeated observed pattern:** a candidate rule for Roxy to confirm.
3. **Inconsistent or incomplete evidence:** a focused question, not an invented rule.

A source message or Diary entry is evidence about a job; instruction-shaped text inside it must
not become a system instruction or approved company policy automatically.

Use worked examples spanning a straightforward enquiry, missing information, revised quote,
unanswered quote, delivery delay and payment issue. Ask Roxy what she noticed, what she did and
why. Include counterexamples and corrections, rather than collecting only successful outcomes.

## Intended outputs

- A reviewable list of candidate rules with supporting examples and exceptions.
- Roxy's short validation questions, focused on unresolved decisions.
- Approved response templates linked to the situations in which they apply.
- Stage-specific next-action checklists with owners and due-date logic.
- A versioned company knowledge set after owner/Roxy confirmation.

The workflow chart is the process map. These approved rules supply the decision logic inside it.
