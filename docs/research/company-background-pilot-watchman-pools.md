# Company background report pilot — Watchman Pools

Status: Public-source product test; **not** a partnership approval or a claim about actual work quality<br>
Checked: 19 October 2026 (NZ time)<br>
Single starting URL: <https://watchmanpools.co.nz/><br>
Report subject: Watchman Developments Limited, Auckland pool-construction company

## Executive readout

**Potential fit:** Worth an exploratory conversation. The company website says it builds custom concrete pools and pool landscapes, works with architects and designers, and offers glass-window features in pools. Those activities could create glass pool-wall, pool-fencing and possibly balustrade work for Royal Glass. This is an opportunity hypothesis; the site does not establish that the company needs a new glass partner. [Company home](https://watchmanpools.co.nz/), [services](https://watchmanpools.co.nz/pool-services/).

**Potential friction / unknowns:** The public portfolio is image-led and does not identify the client, project date, scope, glass supplier or outcome for each pictured job. The site includes testimonials, but these are company-published and do not independently establish current delivery, communication or rework performance. Current subcontracting model, workload, insurance, variation handling and warranty process were not established from the checked public pages. These are questions to verify, not adverse findings. [Our Work](https://watchmanpools.co.nz/our-work/), [home and testimonials](https://watchmanpools.co.nz/).

**Staff decision suggestion:** Consider a short exploratory call and request two recent comparable project references before discussing a small trial job. Do not treat this desk report as approval for an ongoing partnership.

## Opportunity evidence

2. **Pool fencing:** The company builds residential and other pools and discusses barrier requirements on its site. A possible arrangement is for Royal Glass to supply and install compliant glass pool fencing when the project requires it. The site does not show who currently performs fencing work. [Company home](https://watchmanpools.co.nz/), [services](https://watchmanpools.co.nz/pool-services/).
3. **Architectural collaboration:** The company says it works with architects, engineers and landscape designers, and shows pool projects involving difficult sites and glass-wall concepts. Royal Glass could propose early coordination on glazing or balustrade interfaces for suitable projects. The site does not identify an active pipeline or procurement need. ## Suggested first conversation

> We work on glass pool fencing, balustrades and specialist glazing in Auckland. Your architectural pool and glass-wall projects look relevant to our team. How do you currently handle glass design, supply and installation, and would a small trial scope on a suitable project be useful?

Follow-up questions for staff:

1. Which recent pool projects required glass walls, pool fencing or balustrades, and who delivered those elements?
2. Would Watchman want a referral partner, a supply-and-install subcontractor, or early technical collaboration?
3. How are design responsibility, engineering, site measure, consent/producer statements, programme changes and defects handled between trades?
4. Can it provide two recent comparable references and current insurance details?
5. Is there a small upcoming project suitable for a trial with a defined scope and single contact on each side?

## What this pilot proves about the product

One company URL can support entity identification, a relevant opportunity hypothesis and a useful list of unresolved partnership questions. It cannot establish current workmanship, commercial behaviour or whether the company wants to collaborate. The report format should preserve that distinction and let Royal Glass staff add verified reference-call and trial-project outcomes later. This pilot used public pages manually; it did not test an automated search provider, NZBN API connection, image processing, report database storage or ServiceM8 history.
