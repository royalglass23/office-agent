[Company website](https://topviewcon.co.nz/), [company LinkedIn updates](https://www.linkedin.com/company/topview-construction-ltd/).

**Potential friction and uncertainty:** The website's [Projects page](https://topviewcon.co.nz/projects/) currently contains generic New York and New Jersey project titles and placeholder marketing text. Do not attribute those projects or photos to this Auckland company. This is a website-evidence problem, not proof of poor building work. The company-posted updates and homepage testimonials are useful leads for reference checks, but they do not independently establish scope, programme performance, quality, defect handling or collaboration with glazing trades. Its [Contact page](https://topviewcon.co.nz/contact-us/) visibly contains a template-domain email link; confirm the correct business contact before outreach.

**Suggested staff decision:** Verify two recent Auckland project references and ask for a small, defined balustrade or glazing scope. Treat the company as a possible lead, not a vetted long-term partner.

## Opportunity assessment

1. **Residential glass balustrades — strongest lead.** Top View says it delivers residential and multi-unit builds; its own [LinkedIn page](https://www.linkedin.com/company/topview-construction-ltd/) describes a completed West Harbour two-dwelling project. Royal Glass could ask whether upcoming homes have decks, balconies or stair barriers and offer an early design and installation conversation. The source does not establish that these projects used glass or that Top View needs a new supplier.
2. **Builder coordination and supply/install — plausible.** Its [About Us page](https://topviewcon.co.nz/about-us/) emphasises project management, carpentry and consultant engagement. A narrowly scoped subcontract arrangement could cover site measure, glazing design coordination and installation on a specific project. Ask how they currently buy, programme and sign off balustrade work.
3. **Pool fencing — exploratory only.** The checked website and company updates did not substantiate active swimming-pool work. Ask whether pool fencing occurs in its residential pipeline before offering a pool-specific partnership.

## Suggested first conversation

> We supply and install glass balustrades and pool fencing around Auckland. We saw your residential and multi-unit work and wondered how you currently handle balustrade design, supply and installation. If there is a suitable upcoming project, we could review one defined scope together.

Questions for Royal Glass staff:

1. Which two recent Auckland projects can a client or main contractor confirm, and what was Top View's exact scope?
2. Are balustrades, stair glass or pool barriers expected in the upcoming pipeline? Who supplies and installs them today?
3. Who owns design, engineering, consent documentation, site measure, installation sequencing and producer statements?
4. Can Top View provide current insurance, relevant guarantees and its approach to variations and defects?
5. Is there one small project to trial with named contacts, milestones and acceptance criteria on both sides?

## Product lesson from this pilot

A single company name can be resolved to a plausible website and legal entity, but a safe one-URL product must explicitly reject unrelated namesakes and placeholder project pages. It should distinguish self-published project leads from verified references, show image source links without copying image assets by default, and let staff add reference-call/trial outcomes to a dated report version. This was a manual public-source test; it did not exercise automated search, photo interpretation, NZBN API access, report database storage, or ServiceM8 data.
